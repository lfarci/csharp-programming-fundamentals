# Projects

A project is a small program that is **yours**: no tests, no answer key, and you decide how it looks. Each one starts tiny and grows, milestone by milestone, into a complete program. Then the stretch goals let you go as far as you want.

**Terminal games**: Play them, beat your friends, then make them harder.

- [Guessing game](guessing-game.md): The computer hides a number. Hot or cold? Find it in as few tries as you can. *Easy, 1 to 2 hours.*
- [Hangman](hangman.md): Guess the secret word letter by letter before the little man is drawn. *Medium, 3 to 4 hours.*
- [Rock paper scissors+](rock-paper-scissors-lizard-spock.md): The classic game, then the 5-weapon version where Spock vaporizes rock. *Easy, 2 to 3 hours.*
- [Pig dice duel](pig-dice.md): Roll again or bank your points? One 1 and you lose the whole turn. *Medium, 2 to 3 hours.*
- [Tic-tac-toe](tic-tac-toe.md): X against O on a 3x3 grid, then against a computer that never loses. *Hard, 4 to 6 hours.*

**Learn something real**: Small tools that teach you a real-world idea while you code.

- [Weather station](weather-station.md): Turn a week of temperatures into stats, trends and an ASCII chart, like a meteorologist. *Medium, 3 to 4 hours.*
- [What should I wear?](dress-for-the-weather.md): Convert temperatures, feel the wind chill and get outfit advice from your own program. *Easy, 1 to 2 hours.*
- [Expense tracker](expense-tracker.md): Where does your money go? Track expenses by category and see your budget melt. *Medium, 3 to 5 hours.*

**Just for fun**: Stories, quizzes and surprises: the program talks back.

- [Quiz show](text-quiz.md): Host your own TV quiz: jokers, streaks, a timer and a final score. *Easy, 2 to 3 hours.*
- [Tiny dungeon](dungeon-crawler.md): Explore rooms, fight slimes, find the treasure: a text adventure on a map. *Hard, 5 to 8 hours.*

## How to work on a project

1. Pick one that makes you smile. *Easy* ones fit after the Loops theme; *Hard* ones use arrays and methods.
2. Make a new console app **next to** the course folder, not inside it: `dotnet new console -o HangmanGame`, then `cd HangmanGame` and `dotnet run`. Write your code in its `Program.cs`.
3. Do the **milestones in order**. Stuck? Open **one** hint, try again, then the next.
4. Done? Pick stretch goals, show your program to someone and explain how it works.

Want more ideas? Play the small games in Microsoft's [.NET console games](https://github.com/dotnet/dotnet-console-games) collection, pick one, close the code, and write it yourself.
